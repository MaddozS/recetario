# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Food entrepreneurs: pastry chefs, caterers, food truck owners, small-batch producers running food businesses in Spanish-speaking markets. They need to cost recipes accurately to set menu prices, manage margins, and make profitable decisions. Many are sole operators or small teams doing everything themselves — they value speed and clarity over hand-holding.

## Product Purpose

KitchCalc is a recipe costing calculator. It lets food professionals break down ingredient costs per recipe, model presentations (batch sizes), plan production runs, and understand their margins. The output is confident pricing decisions.

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, warm. Three facets:

- **Confident**: The tool trusts the user's expertise. No patronizing tutorials, no excessive validation. Numbers speak for themselves.
- **Precise**: Every calculation is transparent. Costs, margins, and quantities are never hidden or approximated without disclosure.
- **Warm**: The tone is human and approachable — Spanish-first, conversational where appropriate, but never chummy or infantilizing.

## Anti-references

This is NOT:

- **SaaS-cream generic**: No white cards floating on pale gradients, no purple CTAs, no illustrations of people pointing at charts. No hero-metric layouts.
- **Overly technical/enterprise**: No dense Jira-like tables, no monospace-everywhere, no jargon-heavy labels. The user is a cook, not an ERP operator.
- **Playful/childish food apps**: No cartoon vegetables, no rounded-everything, no rainbow palettes or confetti animations.

## Design Principles

1. **Numbers own the page**: Costs, margins, and quantities are the primary citizen. Everything else — chrome, navigation, decoration — recedes. The data is the UI.
2. **Speed of task**: Every interaction optimized for fast data entry and scanning. Keyboard-friendly, minimal clicks, dense but scannable layouts.
3. **Show the math**: Calculations are transparent. Users should never wonder how a number was derived. Margins, fixed costs, unit costs — all traceable.
4. **Familiarity as feature**: Standard product UI patterns (sidebar nav, form layouts, data tables). No invented affordances. The tool disappears into the task.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline: sufficient color contrast, keyboard navigation, semantic HTML, and screen-reader support. Dark mode supported. Accommodations for reduced motion where animation is used.
